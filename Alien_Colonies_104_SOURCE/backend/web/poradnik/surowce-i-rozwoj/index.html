Resources, buildings and research in Alien Colonies

Alien Colonies guide · Updated: 13 October 2026

A productive economy gives you more options. Understand what limits your colony's output before spending resources on another building level.

Development in this space strategy game brings several systems together. Mines produce resources, the power plant supplies energy, the shipyard builds your fleet, and the laboratory enables research. Your next upgrade depends on your empire's plans and the properties of the planet you are developing.

What are metal, crystal and fuel used for?

Metal
A basic construction material. You spend it on buildings, ships, defenses and many research projects. Increase its production by upgrading the metal mine or your race's equivalent.
Crystal
Used for research, construction and your fleet. Crystal mines produce it. Advanced technology paths can require large reserves of this resource.
Fuel
Powers fleet travel and is also part of the cost of many projects. Refineries produce it. Keep a reserve for transport and planned missions while developing your colonies.

Swarm Crystals are a separate, rare resource. You acquire them on expeditions against the Swarm and spend them on skills. They do not replace ordinary crystal in project costs. Expedition loot becomes available when the surviving fleet returns.

Ordinary resource stockpiles belong to individual colonies. If a project costs more than the selected planet holds, you must first transport supplies from another colony. Having resources elsewhere in your empire does not automatically fund a local project.

Why does energy affect all resource production?

The power plant supplies energy, while mines and the refinery consume it. Energy is not a fourth ordinary resource you stockpile alongside metal. It is a balance between supply and demand.

If demand exceeds supply, all three production lines operate at a reduced rate. For example, 100 power against a demand of 125 gives a factor of 0.8: production runs at 80% of the output it would otherwise achieve. Additional bonuses do not remove the power shortage itself.

Check the expected balance before upgrading a mine. A power plant upgrade can sometimes be more useful than another mine level because it restores full production of several resources at once. Energy supply and consumption vary by race; planet traits and technologies can also change them.

Planet type and building fields matter

Planets have different production multipliers. Metallic planets favor metal, crystalline planets favor crystal, and icy planets favor fuel. Higher production of one resource may come with lower output of the others. Rare traits can further change a colony's capabilities.

Check the planet's size and available fields, too. Each building level occupies a field, and an upgrade reserves its space when it starts. Racial buildings and the observatory also use these fields. On a small planet, decide early which roles to prioritize.

Your second colony does not have to copy your first. You can specialize it in the resource your empire lacks most and move supplies between planets. Weigh the benefits against travel and development costs: a distant planet with a good multiplier is not always the most convenient first choice.

What do the basic buildings do?

  • Mines and the refinery increase metal, crystal and fuel production.
  • The power plant supplies energy for resource production.
  • The shipyard produces ships and defenses. Higher levels affect production time and unlock further projects.
  • The laboratory enables research. Its level affects research duration and access to technologies.
  • The observatory reveals information about nearby systems in your galaxy. It requires a level 2 laboratory; higher levels increase the range and detail of observation.

Races have their own building names and special projects. The race comparison covers their base production multipliers and energy balance. For example, the Zetan laboratory is called the Psionic Observatory, but it is a different building from the shared Observatory used to observe the galaxy. Read the description and requirements to identify each building's role.

How do queues work?

A planet can run a building project, shipyard production and research at the same time. Ships and defenses share the shipyard queue. Research has an additional restriction: only one research project can run across the entire empire at once. Starting another from a different colony does not bypass this limit.

The project cost is deducted when work begins. Before confirming, make sure the selected colony has enough resources for the whole order and that you are not occupying a queue needed for something more urgent.

How should you choose research?

Research benefits the entire empire, but you must start it at a colony with the required laboratory and local resources. Advanced technologies can require several completed research levels as well as an appropriate laboratory level.

  • Propulsion adds 15% of base travel speed per level.
  • Weapons increases attack strength by 15% per level.
  • Energy Technology increases power plant output by 6% per level.
  • Computing increases research speed by 5% per level.
  • Robotics increases building construction speed by 4% per level; it requires Computing 3 and Laboratory 3.
  • Nanotechnology increases ordinary resource production by 3% per level; it requires Robotics 5 and Laboratory 5.

Speed bonuses modify the underlying stats; they do not mean that every duration falls by the same percentage. Check the catalog for specific times and effects. Later paths include advanced propulsion, orbital engineering, the Dyson Sphere and wormholes, all of which require earlier stages of development.

Choose research that serves a practical goal. If power is short, consider Energy Technology. If you can afford projects but waiting slows your progress, look at speed technologies. If you plan to expand or fight, weigh propulsion and weapons needs against those of your economy.

A simple checklist before your next upgrade

  1. Choose a goal: another colony, higher production, a new ship class or a specific technology.
  2. Check the cost and requirements in the selected planet's catalog.
  3. Identify resource shortages, check the energy balance and count the available fields.
  4. Account for occupied queues and any transport needed to bring in supplies.
  5. Keep fuel for planned missions, then start the project.
