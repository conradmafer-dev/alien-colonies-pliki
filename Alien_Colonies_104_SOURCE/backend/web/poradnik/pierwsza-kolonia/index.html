Guide / Starting your empire

How to get started in Alien Colonies

Alien Colonies guide · Updated: 13 October 2026

Your first goal is a working colony: resource production with enough power, supplies for development and a fleet built for a specific mission. Use that foundation to grow your empire.

In this space strategy game, you manage colonies and send ships between planets. Creating an empire gives you your first planet with basic buildings and a supply of metal, crystal and fuel. New empires start without ships, so you will need to build your first fleet. Use your Google account to sign in on Android or in the browser.

In this guide

  1. Choose your race
  2. Check production and energy
  3. Use your beginner protection
  4. Prepare a second colony
  5. Scout the Swarm

1. Choose a race that suits your plans

Your race changes the names and stats of buildings and ships. Each race also has its own economic advantages and limitations. Read the description before choosing: no race is best in every situation.

  • Zetans have a crystal production bonus and a strong opening volley. Their light ships are fast, but lower metal production and higher mining energy use need careful management.
  • Symbionts produce more fuel, and their surviving hulls regenerate some damage after a combat round. They produce less crystal; living ships take longer to build and travel more slowly.
  • Automatons have a metal production bonus and reduced incoming damage. Their heavy ships are slower and use more fuel, while fuel production is lower.

Your choice of race is permanent for the empire. Make it before starting projects or sending fleets: ongoing tasks or missions may prevent selection. Before confirming, read the comparison of Zetans, Symbionts and Automatons.

2. Check your energy balance and the next project cost

Metal, crystal and fuel are resources you stockpile and spend. Energy powers their production. If your power plant cannot meet demand, production of all three ordinary resources falls.

Check your energy balance before upgrading a mine. Then choose a project that addresses your current bottleneck: a metal mine when metal is short, a crystal mine for planned research, a refinery before expeditions, or a power plant when demand exceeds supply. Building names depend on your race, but their roles stay the same.

The catalog shows each project's cost, duration and requirements. Use it to guide your decisions instead of following a fixed upgrade order, because production also depends on the planet's type and traits. The resources and development guide explains more.

3. Make the most of your first 24 hours of protection

A new empire receives 24 hours of protection. Use that time to establish production, learn the catalog and scout your surroundings. Attacking another player ends your protection early. Before a PvP mission, decide whether you are ready to expose your colonies to retaliation.

Protection is no reason to neglect defense. Once it expires, your fuel reserves, shipyard capabilities and knowledge of nearby planets become important. If you join an alliance, discuss colony locations and how its members work together.

4. Prepare to establish a second colony

First, meet the shipyard and research requirements shown in the catalog, then build a colonizer. You also need an unoccupied planet known to your empire and enough fuel for the trip. Scouting and observation reveal your surroundings, while planet type information helps you choose a location for future production.

  1. Find an unoccupied target and check its type and available building fields.
  2. Select the colonization mission and a colonizer. You can include other ships and supplies within the fleet's cargo capacity.
  3. Check the quoted travel time, fuel cost and cargo capacity before launch.
  4. Wait for the colony report. The colonizer is consumed to establish the base; the other ships and the resources they carry stay at the new colony.

The planet must still be unoccupied when your fleet arrives. If another player has claimed it or you have reached your colony limit, the expedition returns. An empire can have up to 12 colonies; ongoing colonization missions also count when you launch further expeditions.

A second colony offers new production opportunities, but it needs its own development. Do not empty your first planet if that would leave you without transport fuel or resources for an important project. The second colony guide walks through the browser steps and includes a cargo calculation example.

5. Scout the Khar Swarm before attacking

The Swarm is an alien force in the galaxy. Its ordinary resources and rare Swarm Crystals serve different purposes: the rare crystals are used to develop skills. Do not confuse them with the ordinary crystal spent on buildings and research.

Send a scout and read the report on ships, defenses and stockpiles. A strong hive is not a safe target for a handful of early frigates. Check the target type, the age of your reconnaissance and your fleet's capabilities. If necessary, build up your fleet and discuss the situation with your alliance first. Loot reaches your empire only when the surviving ships return.

Learn how to assess a scout report. For more on missions and preparing for combat, see the fleet and alliance guide.

Three mistakes to avoid

  • Upgrading production without enough energy. Check your power balance before adding another mine level.
  • Spending all your fuel on projects. Keep a reserve for planned missions and transport.
  • Attacking based on an old report. Reconnaissance records the situation at the time of observation; an opponent can later strengthen defenses or move ships.
