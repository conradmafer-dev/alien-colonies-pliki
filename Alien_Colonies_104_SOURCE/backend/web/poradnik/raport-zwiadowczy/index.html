Scouting reports in Alien Colonies: what they tell you

Alien Colonies guide · Updated: 13 October 2026

A scouting report records a target's state when your scout arrives. It helps you prepare for colonization or an attack, but is not a live view of an opponent.

In this guide

  1. How to get a scouting report
  2. What to read first
  3. Scouting and observatories show different information

How to get a scouting report

Open Galaxy and choose a mission target, or use the scouting action for an unexplored part of a system. Select a scout mission and at least one scout ship. The Eye of Zeta, Firefly and Probe 01 have different statistics, but all serve the same reconnaissance role.

Check the route and fuel cost, then send the fleet. The report is created when the scout reaches the target; checking the price or launching does not reveal the target's state. After observing the planet, the scout returns to its colony. Opening an old report does not perform another scouting mission.

In the browser, open Reports and select the scouting report to view its details. Check the planet name, observation time, resources, ships, defenses and building information. You can also open a planet to review its latest scouting report. Similar report titles may refer to different targets or separate observations of the same planet.

What to read first

  1. Target and owner. Establish whether you scouted an unoccupied planet, a player empire or the Khar Swarm. An unoccupied planet may be suitable for colonization; an attack has different requirements.
  2. Observation time. Consider what may have changed since the report. Resources may have accumulated or been spent, and ships may have departed or returned.
  3. Resource stocks. Read metal, crystals and fuel separately. These are stockpiles at a particular moment, not guaranteed loot.
  4. Fleet. Check individual ship types and quantities. The total ship count does not describe attack power, hull strength or fleet roles.
  5. Planetary defenses. Check these separately. A planet with no ships in orbit can still be defended.

Different races use different ship names. Compare roles and statistics rather than assuming an unfamiliar name means a weaker ship. The race comparison explains the basics of each race's economy and combat traits.

Read beyond the summary: open the report to inspect the available details. Do not treat information omitted from a summary or an older report as zero: a missing number does not prove that a planet has no defenses or resources.

Scouting and observatories show different information

Scouting saves an observation of the planet and information about its system. Reading that report later does not update its resources, fleets or defenses. Even when the planet name is familiar, check where the information came from and how old it is.

An observatory provides local observation coverage in the galaxy containing your colonies. The information available improves as you develop the building. A “Live observation” marker on the map does not mean an old scouting report has acquired fresh resource and ship counts.

Scout again before an important mission, especially after a long gap, a battle or a change of ownership. There is no single safe report age for every situation. How quickly intelligence becomes outdated depends on player actions, fleet movements and the planet's development.

Example: no fleet does not mean a safe attack

Suppose a summary lists a player, three resource stockpiles, “no ships” and several defense units. The conclusion is limited: no ships were stationed there when the scout arrived, but planetary defenses existed. The report cannot predict what will arrive before your attacking fleet.

First compare the observation's age with your planned arrival time. Then check your own ships, fuel and cargo capacity. One sentence in a report cannot guarantee a battle's outcome. This example illustrates how to interpret intelligence, not a promise of victory or a particular reward.

If you found an unoccupied planet instead of another player's empire, the decision changes: you need a colonizer and supplies for development. See the second-colony guide.

What to check when scouting the Khar Swarm

The Swarm has different kinds of outposts. A strong hive and a weaker foothold are not equivalent targets. Detailed intelligence may include fleets, defenses, Swarm Crystal stocks and observed activity. All of it describes the time of observation, even when the wording sounds like an ongoing action.

Swarm Crystals are a separate resource used to develop skills. Do not confuse them with ordinary crystals. The amount observed does not mean your fleet will bring all of it home: combat results, reward rules and surviving ships' cargo capacity matter.

If the information available about an outpost is incomplete or old, scout again before judging its strength. Do not fill those gaps by assuming the target is weak. Read the fleet and alliance guide for mission planning, fuel and cooperation.
