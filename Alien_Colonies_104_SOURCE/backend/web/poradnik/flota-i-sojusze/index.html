Fleets, scouting and alliances in Alien Colonies

Alien Colonies guide · Updated: 13 October 2026

Every expedition needs a destination, information and fuel. Choose ships for the task, check the flight quote, then give the order.

Your fleet lets you act beyond your own colony. Ships carry out reconnaissance, transport, colonization and combat missions. Each race has different ship names and stats, so compare their roles, speed, cargo capacity, hull strength and attack power in your catalog.

What is available in the browser? You can send your own scouting, transport, colonization and attack missions, and use basic alliance features and alliance chat. The browser client does not yet include panels for joint attacks or defensive support. The later sections explain how these operations work on the shared server; issuing those orders requires a client with the relevant panel.

Match your fleet to the mission

Scout
Requires at least one scout ship. Reveals the target's resources, ships and defenses, and records information about the surrounding area.
Transport
Carries ordinary resources to your own colony or an ally's colony. The ships return after delivering their cargo. If the destination is no longer owned by you or an ally when the fleet arrives, the transport turns back.
Colonize
Requires a colonizer and an unoccupied planet. The colonizer becomes the new colony's base; other ships and supplies brought along stay there. If the target is no longer available, the fleet returns.
Attack
Sends your fleet into combat against the Swarm or another player. Check the target's defenses and protection, and decide what losses you can afford. You cannot attack your own colony or an alliance member.

Cargo ships carry supplies, colonizers establish colonies, and warships provide combat strength. A larger fleet is not always better for the task: unnecessary ships can increase fuel costs or slow the journey.

Check the requirements before building new classes. Shipyard levels and research unlock more options; advanced units also require you to choose a race. The second colony guide covers colonization step by step. If your economy needs preparation, read the buildings and research guide.

Reconnaissance provides information, and every report has a date

The galaxy does not reveal the full state of every planet immediately. The observatory provides local observation of systems in your galaxy, while scouting records reconnaissance. Before sending a mission other than scouting, the target must be known to your empire.

A scout report is a snapshot of the situation at the time of observation. Stockpiles, defenses and ships can change afterward. An old report helps with planning but does not guarantee the planet's current state.

Before attacking, check the reconnaissance time, defenses and target type. If the report is old or you are planning a major expedition, scout again. Joining an alliance does not reveal the entire enemy galaxy. The scout report guide explains what you can infer from a report and which details the browser client displays.

Check fuel cost, travel time and cargo capacity

Before launch, check the mission quote for your chosen route and fleet composition. The quoted fuel cost covers the round trip. Propulsion research, racial stats and the ships you select affect travel time; distant routes can require much larger reserves.

Cargo cannot exceed the fleet's capacity. You can carry ordinary resources on transport and colonization missions. For combat expeditions, leave enough cargo capacity to bring back loot, but do not sacrifice the combat strength needed to break through the defenses.

Ships in transit are unavailable at the colony for another mission. Some may be destroyed in combat, and loot only becomes available after the survivors return.

How should you prepare an expedition against the Khar Swarm?

Scout the target first. The galaxy contains different Swarm outposts, and a strong hive is a very different challenge from a weak foothold. Winning at one location does not mean you are ready for every other target.

Swarm Crystals are rare loot used to develop skills. Check the reported stockpile and prepare both combat strength and room for loot. The amount you can bring home depends on the target's state, combat rules and the cargo capacity of your surviving ships.

Check fresh reconnaissance before planning another raid instead of relying only on the outcome of the previous battle. The Swarm can also attack player colonies for ordinary resources, so preparing your defenses remains important.

Alliances let you plan together

An alliance can have up to 20 members. It includes leader, officer and member roles, recruitment and private chat. Applications and invitations require acceptance. Alliance membership also lets you send resource transports to an ally's colony.

Before joining, find out how the group coordinates attacks and defensive support. Leaving, being removed or dissolving an alliance triggers a 24-hour mutual truce and a 24-hour wait before you can join an alliance again.

How do joint attacks work on the server?

The browser client does not yet have a panel for this type of operation. The ordinary Attack action in the Galaxy panel sends your fleet on an individual mission.

The organizer chooses a known target and a shared arrival time. An alliance can have up to 3 active plans; up to 5 empires can participate in one attack, with one fleet each.

  • The attack can be scheduled from 5 minutes to 24 hours ahead.
  • Joining closes 30 seconds before the attack.
  • Each joining fleet must be able to reach the target by the shared arrival time.
  • Ships retain the stats of their own race and owner.

Agree on fleet composition, fuel and participant availability before launch. Rare Swarm Crystals have a shared limit per battle and are divided among participants; a joint attack does not multiply that limit by the number of empires. Each owner's loot becomes available when their fleet returns.

Supporting an ally's colony defenses

The server supports the rules below, but the browser client does not currently provide a panel for sending this type of operation.

One defended planet can receive support from up to 5 empires. Fleets can remain on duty for 1, 4 or 8 hours after arrival. Their presence is temporary, so choose a period that matches the expected threat.

Alliance operations use ordinary flight rather than a wormhole. Fuel for the round trip and the stationing period is paid at launch; recalling the fleet does not refund it. Check the quote, arrival time and your own defensive reserves before sending every available ship to another colony.

Before giving the order

  1. Choose the mission and confirm the destination.
  2. Check the report's age, the target's owner and its protection.
  3. Select ships, cargo and a fuel reserve.
  4. Read the travel time, cost and cargo capacity quote.
  5. For alliance operations, confirm the shared timing and your fleet's role.
