Which race should you choose in Alien Colonies?

Alien Colonies guide · Updated: 13 October 2026

Your race shapes your empire's economy and fleet. The choice is permanent, so weigh each race's strengths and limitations before starting your first projects.

Comparing production under the same conditions

Zetans, Symbionts and Automatons all use metal, crystals and fuel, but have different production multipliers. This comparison isolates the effect of race: assume the same planet, mining levels, technologies and other bonuses, with enough energy for full production. These figures are not a forecast of a new account's income.

Race multipliers relative to base production
RaceMetalCrystalsFuel
Zetans90%125%100%
Symbionts100%85%120%
Automatons125%100%80%

For example, if crystal production before the race multiplier is 100 units per hour, Zetans produce 125, Symbionts 85 and Automatons 100. This does not mean every mine has a base output of 100. Check your colony's actual production in the game, as energy, environment and development also affect it.

Zetans: crystals and a powerful opening volley

Zetans produce 25% more crystals but 10% less metal. Crystals support research and construction, but this advantage does not remove the need for other resources. When planning several projects, compare their full costs rather than looking only at your largest stockpile.

Their opening volley multiplies attack by 1.35. Later combat rounds do not receive this opening bonus. Light discs have fast drives but weaker hulls, so a strong opening does not guarantee victory in a longer battle.

The Gravity Extractor uses 23 energy per level; the Crystal Resonator and Ion Distiller each use 29. The Singularity Core provides a base 100 energy per level. Keep checking your power balance as you upgrade mines.

Symbionts: fuel and regeneration for surviving ships

Symbionts produce 20% more fuel and 15% fewer crystals. Extra fuel can support more expeditions, but research still needs crystals. Choosing a suitable planet and upgrading the Silicon Garden can help compensate for the shortfall.

After each combat round, surviving hulls heal 35% of their damage. Destroyed ships do not regrow. Living ships take longer to build and have slower base drives, which matters when planning distant missions.

The Colony Heart provides a base 85 energy per level. Iron Roots use 17, while the Silicon Garden and Fuel Gland each use 21. Lower energy output therefore comes with different mining energy requirements.

Automatons: metal and tough hulls

Automatons produce 25% more metal but 20% less fuel. Their armor reduces incoming damage by 12%. Heavy units have strong hulls, but travel more slowly and use more fuel.

Before expanding your fleet, compare its construction cost with the fuel needed to use it. A large metal stockpile cannot launch a mission if the starting colony lacks fuel for both the journey and its cargo.

The Industrial Reactor provides a base 120 energy per level. The Drilling Complex uses 25, while the Silicon Separator and Synthetic Cracker each use 31. Higher reactor output does not automatically mean a larger energy surplus.

Choosing before your first project

No race is best in every situation. Consider production, energy needs, ship statistics, travel time and fuel costs together. Technology levels, an opponent's development and fleet composition can matter more to a battle than a single racial bonus.

  1. Read each race's benefits and limitations on the selection screen.
  2. Choose the economy and fleet style you want to develop.
  3. Check that no active construction or mission is preventing the choice.
  4. After confirming, open the catalog to see your race's names and current costs.

Your choice belongs to your empire on the shared server. Switching between Android and the browser does not let you choose again. Active projects, your own flights or missions heading to your planets can prevent the initial selection; once confirmed, it cannot be freely changed.
